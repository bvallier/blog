I built a product that already exists. It is called WorkDive—an SMS-based time-tracking app for contractors and small businesses. I still genuinely like using it, though building something yourself makes it surprisingly hard to admit when the market doesn't actually need it.

## Where the Idea Came From

At 22, I ran my first small business, a math and science tutoring shop called TutorDudes with about twenty tutors. Business was good, but payroll was painful. I duct-taped Formstack to spreadsheets and manually sent individual PayPal payouts every cycle.

Years later, with far more engineering experience under my belt, I revisited the problem. Within the first week of building WorkDive, two realities hit me: time-tracking apps are everywhere, and [Harvest](https://www.getharvest.com/) had already spent years perfecting the category—and I hadn't even looked at their site before writing code (**mistake #1**).

## A Chain of Rationalizations

I kept building anyway, telling myself it would be a worthwhile learning exercise even if it never made a dollar (**mistake #2**). The initial version was simple: text a user to log their hours, and organize the entries into revenue, cost, and profit dashboards.

Then a stranger signed up for the trial and actually paid $9/month. That single conversion convinced me I was onto something and sent me straight back into feature-building mode (**mistake #3**).

When I finally studied Harvest, I was floored. Their UX, invoicing, and integrations were light-years ahead—and mirrored almost everything on my roadmap. Somehow I spun even that as validation (**mistake #4**).

## Facing the Numbers (and My Doppelgänger)

Reading an interview with Harvest CEO Danny Wen brought things into sharp focus. Not only had I built a tiny subset of his product differentiated only by SMS entry, I vaguely looked like the guy.

Harvest had logged over 122,000,000 hours. WorkDive had logged 473.26 hours—roughly **0.00039%** of their volume. When I tried pitching a Harvest user, she politely emailed asking to be removed from the SMS reminders. Message received.

## What I Took Away

Comparing a solo side project to an established market leader isn't unfair—customers make that comparison automatically. WorkDive still works nicely as my personal time-tracker, so I'm keeping it running while shifting my energy toward problems with clearer differentiation.

Three lessons stuck with me:

- **Listen when someone close to you points out a competitor:** When your spouse casually mentions a "cool product" that sounds a lot like yours, check it out before writing another line of code.
- **Be honest about the competitive landscape:** Building to learn is great, as long as you notice the moment learning plateaus and revenue isn't coming.
- **Domain expertise is the real IP:** Developers love saying execution beats ideas, and execution certainly matters—but without deep domain insight into a specific, underserved problem, you end up writing clean code for a product nobody needs to switch to.
